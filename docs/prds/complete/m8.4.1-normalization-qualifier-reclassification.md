## 1. Problem Statement

After M8.4's ML-powered parsing pipeline was complete, user testing revealed that "ground beef" was being normalized to just "beef". The 3-tier parser (regex → ML → NLP) correctly produced `name: "ground beef"` — the bug was downstream in `IngredientTemplateService.normalize()`.

### Root Cause

Phase 4 of `normalize()` — `removeVariations()` — maintained a flat list of ~30+ qualifier words to strip from ingredient names. This list conflated two fundamentally different types of qualifiers:

| Type | Examples | Effect | Should Strip? |
|------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Preparation qualifiers** | diced, chopped, sliced, minced | Describe what you DO to an ingredient | ✅ Yes |
| **Identity qualifiers** | ground, fresh, frozen, dried, dark, whole, unsalted | Change WHAT the ingredient IS | ❌ No |

"Ground beef" and "beef" are different products found in different sections of the store. "Diced tomatoes" and "tomatoes" are the same product — one is just pre-cut.

### Impact

Any ingredient with an identity qualifier was being collapsed to its base form:
- "ground beef" → "beef"
- "ground turkey" → "turkey"
- "ground cinnamon" → "cinnamon"
- "dark chocolate" → "chocolate"
- "whole wheat flour" → "wheat flour"
- "unsalted butter" → "butter"
- "frozen peas" → "peas"
- "dried cranberries" → "cranberry"
- "fresh basil" → "basil"

This caused incorrect template deduplication (ground beef merged with beef) and confusing grocery list entries.

## 2. Decision: Data-Driven Qualifier Reclassification

### Why Not a Hand-Curated Compound List?

The initial fix attempt was to maintain a `preservedCompounds` static set of known compound ingredients (ground beef, dark chocolate, etc.). This approach was rejected because:

1. **Incomplete coverage** — no way to know all compound ingredients without exhaustive enumeration
2. **Maintenance burden** — every new compound ingredient requires a code change
3. **Available training data** — the strangetom dataset (68,846 samples) already encodes which qualifiers are part of ingredient names

### Data Mining the Training Set

A Python analysis of the strangetom training data extracted 3,032 unique compound ingredients where qualifier words are labeled as NAME (part of the ingredient), not PREP/COMMENT (modifiers to strip).

Key findings from the training data:

| Qualifier | Occurrences as NAME | Top Compounds |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| ground | 838 | ground cinnamon (79), ground cumin (63), ground beef (46) |
| whole | 940 | whole milk (125), whole wheat flour (42) |
| fresh | 4,523 | fresh cilantro (268), fresh basil (152), fresh ginger (143) |
| dried | 1,227 | dried thyme (138), dried oregano (106), dried basil (72) |
| unsalted | 904 | unsalted butter (797) |
| frozen | 568 | frozen corn (76), frozen peas (68), frozen spinach (52) |
| dark | 384 | dark chocolate (108), dark brown sugar (87) |

The overwhelming evidence: these qualifiers are integral to ingredient identity in real-world recipe language.

### The Reclassification

Instead of maintaining a list of exceptions, the fix reclassifies qualifiers at the category level:

**Before (30+ qualifiers stripped):**
```
diced, chopped, sliced, minced, crushed, grated, shredded, halved, quartered,
ground, whole, dark, light, heavy, fresh, frozen, canned, dried, raw, organic,
free-range, grass-fed, wild-caught, all-purpose, self-rising, unsalted, salted,
extra-virgin, lite, large, medium, small, jumbo
```

**After (9 preparation-only qualifiers stripped):**
```
diced, chopped, sliced, minced, crushed, grated, shredded, halved, quartered
```

The principle: **only strip qualifiers that describe a physical cutting/processing action that doesn't change the product's identity for shopping purposes.**

## 3. Key Decisions

### Decision 1: Reclassify Rather Than Allowlist

**Choice**: Reduce the strip list to 9 preparation qualifiers rather than maintaining a compound allowlist.

**Rationale**: The training data shows that the vast majority of qualifiers are identity-forming. Stripping should be the exception, not the rule. A short strip list is easier to audit and less likely to break.

**Trade-off**: Some preparation qualifiers we removed from the strip list (like "large" for "large eggs") are now preserved. This is correct — "large eggs" is a distinct product from "eggs" (egg cartons are labeled by size).

### Decision 2: Align normalizePlural() Prefix Stripping

**Choice**: Updated `normalizePlural()`'s `qualifierPrefixes` to match the same 9 preparation qualifiers.

**Rationale**: Both functions must agree on which qualifiers to strip. If `normalizePlural()` strips "ground" but `removeVariations()` doesn't (or vice versa), the pipeline produces inconsistent results depending on input order.

### Decision 3: Add Compound preferPlural Check

**Choice**: Added a last-word lookup against the `preferPlural` map for multi-word ingredient names.

**Rationale**: "Dried cranberries" should stay plural (cranberries is in `preferPlural`), but the existing check only matched single-word entries. The new check extracts the last word of compounds ("cranberries" from "dried cranberries") and applies the plural preference.

### Decision 4: Trust the Parser, Fix the Normalizer

**Choice**: No changes to the 3-tier parser (regex, ML, NLP). All fixes in `IngredientTemplateService.normalize()`.

**Rationale**: The parsers correctly produce `name: "ground beef"`. The ML model was trained on 68,846 samples that label "ground" as NAME in "ground beef" contexts. The normalization layer was undoing the parser's correct work.

## 5. Normalization Pipeline (Post-Fix)

The 5-phase normalize pipeline now operates as:

```
Input: "Diced Ground Beef"
  ↓
Phase 0 (sanitize): strip leading punctuation, unit words → "Diced Ground Beef"
  ↓
Phase 1 (case): lowercase → "diced ground beef"
  ↓
Phase 2 (plural): normalize singular/plural → "diced ground beef"
  ↓
Phase 3 (abbreviations): expand abbreviations → "diced ground beef"
  ↓
Phase 4 (variations): strip PREPARATION qualifiers only → "ground beef"
  ↓
Output: "ground beef"
```

The critical change is Phase 4: only 9 preparation qualifiers are stripped. All identity qualifiers pass through unchanged.
